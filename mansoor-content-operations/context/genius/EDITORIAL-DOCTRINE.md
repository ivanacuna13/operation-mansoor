# Editorial doctrine

## Selection equals promise plus delivery

A moment is not a clip merely because it sounds good. The opening must promise value and the ending must deliver it. The preferred close lands the source video's deeper value proposition and sounds complete.

## Message engineering

The editor is not extracting a contiguous quote and stopping. The editor is engineering a message from the source's intended meaning. Remove words or passages that do not advance the main promise while preserving truth, logic, and natural speech.

## Value per second

Cut:

- filler words and transition scaffolding such as unnecessary `now`, `so`, and `because the principle here is the following`;
- pauses and dead air;
- repeated versions of the same point;
- incomplete examples;
- excessive examples that only reinforce an already clear statement;
- additive phrases that prepare to add value but do not add it.

Keep:

- the clearest formulation of the idea;
- necessary causal links;
- enough context for standalone understanding;
- the promised delivery;
- phrasing that preserves the speaker's voice and intended claim.

## Mechanical truth

Transcript-based editing proposes semantic cuts. Waveform inspection locates the actual boundary. Playback determines whether the new transition is humanly seamless. All three are required.

## Quantity

Find as many clips as meet the contract. Do not divide one coherent idea into incomplete fragments to increase the number. Overlapping candidates are allowed only when their promises and deliveries are materially different.
