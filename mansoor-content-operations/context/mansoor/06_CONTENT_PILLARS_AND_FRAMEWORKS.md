# 06 - CONTENT PILLARS AND FRAMEWORKS

## THE THREE PILLARS

Every piece of content maps to one of three pillars, applied to one of two ICPs. That gives six content tracks total. The client explicitly stated this structure: identity, skill set, strategy, times two (one for the builder/recruiter, one for the producer/lone wolf).

### Pillar 1: Identity

Content that changes how the agent sees themselves and their business. This is where the Marczell and Tony Robbins influence comes in. The goal is to shift their identity from "salesperson" or "recruiter" to "business owner who builds systems that compound."

Key identity shifts:

-   From chasing recognition to chasing take-home income.
    
-   From "I am a sales beast" to "I am a machine that needs a factory around it."
    
-   From "recruiting is beneath me" to "duplication is the only path to passive income."
    
-   From "I need 300 agents" to "I need 60 deployed correctly."
    
-   From "production matters" to "income and persistency matter."
    

### Pillar 2: Skill Set

Content that teaches the missing half. For ICP 1 (recruiters): sales training, lead acquisition, scripts, presentations, objections, professional sales routines. For ICP 2 (lone wolves): duplication systems, recruiting scripts, leadership development, team deployment.

### Pillar 3: Strategy

Content that lays out the path. The stage ladder. The 90-day plan. The math of passive income. The go-wide-go-deep team building strategy. The three-layers-deep framework for hitting $1M/month in production.

## THE FRAMEWORK LIBRARY

The client wants original, named frameworks that serve as containers for existing concepts. These are the mental models that build reality in the viewer's mind. Every framework should have a name the industry has never heard.

### Framework 1: The Leverage Matrix

Container for: the idea that a sales force is a sales force, and deploying it in a different vehicle changes the financial outcome.  
Borrowed from: Hormozi's leverage framework (capital, media, code, people), adapted to insurance.

### Framework 2: The Four D's of Duplication

Container for: the recruiting and team-building process, renamed so salespeople do not reject it.  
Replaces: "recruiting" and "building."

### Framework 3: The Art of Scaling

Container for: the combined sales-plus-recruiting model that the client teaches.  
Replaces: "building an agency" or "growing a team."

### Framework 4: The Leverage Blueprint

Container for: the stage ladder and the progression from lone wolf to leader of leaders.  
Replaces: "compensation plan" or "career path."

### Framework 5: The Passive Income Formula

Container for: the math that shows how many agents at what production level generates what income.  
This is the core of the lead magnet calculator.

### Framework 6: The Carbon Copy Method

Container for: teaching your team to do what you do, by having them watch you do it.  
Replaces: "training" and "onboarding."

### Framework 7: The Go-Wide-Go-Deep Strategy

Container for: the team deployment method where you personally go wide (recruit many) but go deep with the top 3 (develop leaders who develop leaders).  
Three layers deep equals over $1M/month in production.

## THE EXTERNAL INFLUENCE LAYER

The client wants to import concepts from outside the insurance industry and apply them using new containers. These are the source materials he wants woven into the content.

### Marczell (High-Performance Coaching)

Source: Platinum program, coaching calls for high performers.  
Concepts to import:

-   Your future determines who you are today, not the other way around. Most people think what you do today determines your future. In reality, the future you see for yourself determines who you are today.
    
-   Emotional state determines everything (Tony Robbins principle, reinforced by Marczell).
    
-   Emotional noise: the distractions that derail you from your goal.
    
-   Resonance: being aligned with the goal you originally set.
    
-   The proximity principle: closer goals are more motivating than distant ones.
    
-   The container technique: put a concept into a new container to change how it is received (cigarette into raw chicken).
    

Application to insurance: "Think about your best month and your worst month. In your best month, you were locked into a future where you were making $100K/month. In your worst month, something derailed your emotional state. You gave up on the goal. It was not a skill problem. It was a resonance problem."

### Alex Hormozi

Source: Frameworks and mental models, $100M Offers, content strategy.  
Concepts to import:

-   Frameworks as a content structure. Name things. Containerize.
    
-   Leverage: capital, media, code, people. Adapted to insurance: leads, content, systems, agents.
    
-   Value density in content.
    

### Tony Robbins

Source: Emotional state principles.  
Concepts to import:

-   Your emotional state determines your outcomes.
    
-   Change your psychology, change your life.
    
-   Coaching the elite of the elite.
    

### Wes Watson

Source: High-level personal content that attracts elite audiences.  
Concepts to import:

-   Deeply personal, painful content attracts the highest-caliber people. The post about hating himself at the gym attracted billionaires, A-level people, celebrities.
    
-   The merge of unexpected elements: a guy who looks like a convict speaking about stoicism, Marcus Aurelius, Maslow's hierarchy. The contrast is the hook.
    
-   Application: merge high-performance coaching language with insurance agent language. Nobody in insurance talks about emotional noise, resonance, timelines. That language signals elite to the right audience.
    

### Calloway

Source: Content recording method.  
Technique to import:

-   Line-by-line recording for short form. Read one line, look at camera, deliver, look down, read next line, deliver. Cuts are invisible because the presenter is stationary.
    
-   Fully scripted short form. The client is comfortable with this method.

## CONTENT FORMAT STRATEGY

### Short Form (Primary Needle-Mover)

-   Fully scripted, teleprompter, line-by-line recording method (Calloway style).
    
-   30 videos total: 15 targeting ICP 1 (recruiter), 15 targeting ICP 2 (lone wolf).
    
-   Each video drives to a lead magnet via DM CTA: "DM me the word \[X\] and I'll send you \[Y\]."
    
-   Lower views are expected and acceptable. The two people who DM are the right people.
    
-   High volume is the goal. Film in batches with outfit changes.
    

### YouTube Long Form

-   Less scripted than short form, but follows a structured outline.
    
-   Reduce volume of long form. Focus on quality and targeting.
    
-   Long form also drives to lead magnets.
    
-   This is where the client sees his "alpha" and competitive edge.
    

### Live Content and Sales Content (Top of Funnel)

-   Still has value. Not shutting it down.
    
-   Keep doing live and sales-focused content for top of funnel.
    
-   But the bottom of funnel (scripted short form) is where the conversion happens.
    

### The Funnel Architecture

Top of funnel: live content, sales content, YouTube long form (broad, US-filtered).  
Middle of funnel: scripted short form targeting specific ICPs, driving to lead magnets.  
Bottom of funnel: lead magnet (calculator or diagnostic) to booked call.  
Post-call: follow-up sequence (to be designed).
