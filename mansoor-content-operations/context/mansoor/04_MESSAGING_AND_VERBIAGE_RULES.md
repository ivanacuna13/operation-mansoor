# 04 - MESSAGING AND VERBIAGE RULES

This is the most critical document in the entire spine. The client was explicit and repeated: the wrong word will alienate half the industry instantly. Every script, every ad, every email, every landing page must pass these rules.

---

## FORBIDDEN WORDS (NEVER USE)

### "Recruiting"
Why: When an insurance agent hears "recruiting," they think you are going to ask them to hit up their friends, family, and warm market. That is exactly why they left or why they are producing and do not want to do it. The word is poisoned by the MLM/network marketing culture.

### "Building"
Why: Same problem. "Building" sounds like the MLM team-building thing. Salespeople hear it and think "ew, those people just recruit their friends and family, they cannot make money without milking their network."

### "Producer" (as a label for the audience)
Why: It alienates half the industry. The PHP/Primerica/WFG guys do not call themselves producers. That is not their verbiage. If you say producer, you immediately close off the entire recruiting-culture segment.

### "Builder" (as a label for the audience)
Why: It alienates the salespeople. They associate builders with the warm market recruiting crowd.

### "Mindset" and "Motivation"
Why: The client explicitly said less of this. The audience wants selling systems, recruiting systems, and leadership development. They do not want mindset content. They are operators, not dreamers.

### "Flashy lifestyle" framing
Why: The client wants to attract polished, professional, clean-cut people like Pinnacle Financial Group does. Flashy lifestyle, twerking, rented cars, and generic biz-op framing repel the caliber of person he wants.

### Naming Other Companies Directly
Why: Never call out another company by name in any script, ad, email, or landing page. No PHP, no Primerica, no WFG, no FFL, no Symmetry, no competitor names of any kind. Refer to them by their model type only ("the warm-market model companies," "the lead-buying model agencies," "the network marketing culture"). Naming a company directly invites legal exposure and cease-and-desist risk. The audience knows exactly which companies you mean from the description. You do not need the name for them to feel called out, and the name is what creates the liability.

## THE CONTAINER PRINCIPLE

The core messaging technique: take a concept the audience hates or has trauma around, and put it into a new container so they can consume it without their defenses going up.

This comes from Marczell's technique (the cigarette put into a raw chicken container to make a smoker quit). Applied to insurance:

- Take recruiting and put it into the container of leverage or strategy.
- Take sales and lead-buying and put it into the container of client acquisition and revenue-driven activities.
- Take team-building and put it into the container of duplication and passive income engineering.

The medicine is wrapped in candy. Recruiting people need sales but hate sales. Sales people need recruiting but hate recruiting. The content wraps each in the other's language.

---

## UNIQUE MECHANISMS AND FRAMEWORKS (NAMING)

The client wants to invent original framework names that the industry has never heard. These are containers for existing concepts, renamed to create intrigue and ownership. Examples discussed:

- The Leverage Matrix
- The Four C's of Leverage (borrowing from Hormozi's leverage framework: capital, media, code, people)
- The Four D's of Duplication
- The Art of Scaling
- The Leverage Blueprint

The point is not that these are real new systems. The point is that they are new containers for information the audience needs, packaged so they cannot dismiss it as "I already know this." The allure is the unfamiliar name. Who is this guy and what is the leverage matrix?

The client studied QBD and Mario specifically for this technique. He also studied Hormozi's reliance on frameworks as a content structure. He wrote Matt's most viewed life insurance video by taking existing content and reorganizing it into new containers on a whiteboard.

## THE PROXIMITY PRINCIPLE (NUMBER FRAMING)

Never use $1M/month as the primary hook. Use $100K/month.

Reasoning: The closer something is to the prospect, the more motivated they are to go get it. If you tell someone they will have a million subs by the end of the year, they move. If you say in 10 years, they do not. These guys were door-to-door or working random jobs 6 to 12 months ago. They cannot wrap their heads around $1M/month. They can wrap their heads around $100K/month because they have seen people do it.

YouTube data supports this: "millionaire" in a title outperforms "billionaire" because people cannot conceive of a billion. Same principle.

When the client references $1M/month, he means $1M/month in production, not personal income. Always clarify: we are talking about income, not production. The target is $100K/month in take-home income.

---

## INCOME VS PRODUCTION LANGUAGE RULE

Never celebrate production numbers. Never use production as a goal or a milestone.

The line that cuts through: "Who cares about production? How much did you take home?"

Production is a vanity metric that companies use to keep agents chasing recognition. The only numbers that matter are take-home income, persistency, and passive income. Every piece of content should frame the outcome as income, not production.

---

## THE "WRAP THE MEDICINE IN CANDY" RULE

Every piece of content must identify which medicine the audience needs (sales training for recruiters, recruiting systems for salespeople) and which candy to wrap it in (leverage language for recruiters, client acquisition language for salespeople).

If a video is targeting ICP 1 (the recruiter), the medicine is sales training. The candy is leverage and passive income. Never say "you need to learn to sell." Say "you need to deploy your sales force in a vehicle that compounds."

If a video is targeting ICP 2 (the lone wolf), the medicine is duplication and recruiting. The candy is carbon copies and selling in front of an audience. Never say "you need to recruit." Say "you need to create carbon copies of yourself so you are not the only one producing."

---

## GEOGRAPHIC FILTERING RULE

All content must reference US-specific insurance concepts so the algorithm naturally filters the audience. Reference national debt, 401k, US carriers, US lead markets, US licensing. Do not reference generic or international concepts. The goal is that the content is not even a thought to someone in Saudi Arabia or India.

This is validated: Hey Dominic and Dan Henry both live in Dubai but have 80% US-based clients because their messaging only resonates with a US audience.

---

## TONE RULES

- Direct response marketing tone, not personal branding tone. The client explicitly said he views this less like personal branding and more like direct response marketing.
- Value-dense. Every line must carry weight. No filler, no hype, no generic motivation.
- Framework-driven. Structure content around named frameworks and mental models.
- Story-led. Use the client's origin story (leaving PHP, seeing 60 guys out-earn 300) as the narrative spine.
- Conversational but scripted. Short form is fully scripted and read via teleprompter. Long form is less scripted but follows a structured outline.
